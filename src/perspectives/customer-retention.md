<div class="row"><div class="col" style="--span: 12; --of: 12"><div class="b b--html"><h4><strong>What is Customer Retention?</strong></h4><p>Customer retention refers to a company's ability to maintain its existing customers over a sustained period. It is a critical measure of customer satisfaction, product-market fit, and overall business health. High retention rates indicate strong customer loyalty and consistent revenue, which are key drivers of SaaS company valuations.</p><h4><strong>Why Retention Metrics Matter for Companies and Buyers</strong></h4><p>Retention metrics provide insights into the stability and growth potential of a SaaS company. For founders, improving customer retention leads to more predictable revenue, enhanced customer lifetime value, and increased profitability. For potential buyers or investors, these metrics reveal the long-term viability of a company and help assess its scalability and sustainability. A SaaS business with high retention rates is often viewed as a lower-risk, higher-value acquisition target.</p><h4><strong>Key Retention Metrics, How to Calculate Them, and Good Benchmarks</strong></h4><p>Note: The benchmarks provided assume a B2B SaaS context. Generally, enterprise-focused SaaS companies have higher benchmarks compared to SMB-focused companies, while consumer SaaS typically has significantly lower benchmarks.</p><p><strong>Gross Dollar Retention (GDR)</strong></p><ul><li><p><strong>Definition:</strong> GDR measures the percentage of revenue retained from existing customers, excluding expansion revenue (upsells, cross-sells, and upgrades).</p></li><li><p><strong>Formula:</strong> GDR = (Revenue at Start of Period - Revenue Lost Due to Churn) / Revenue at Start of Period * 100%</p></li><li><p><strong>Benchmark:</strong> A GDR of 85-95% is considered strong for SaaS companies.</p></li><li><p><strong><em>Note:</em></strong><em> This metric is arguably the most important and most scrutinized by buyers among all retention metrics.</em></p></li></ul><p><strong>Net Dollar Retention (NDR)</strong></p><ul><li><p><strong>Definition:</strong> NDR measures the percentage of revenue retained from existing customers, including expansion revenue.</p></li><li><p><strong>Formula:</strong> NDR = (Revenue at Start of Period + Expansion Revenue - Revenue Lost Due to Churn) / Revenue at Start of Period * 100%</p></li><li><p><strong>Benchmark:</strong> Best-in-class SaaS companies have NDR above 110-120%, indicating strong growth within the existing customer base.</p></li></ul><p><strong>Logo Retention</strong></p><ul><li><p><strong>Definition:</strong> The percentage of customers (logos) retained over a given period, regardless of revenue fluctuations.</p></li><li><p><strong>Formula:</strong> Logo Retention = (Customers at Start of Period - Customers Lost) / Customers at Start of Period * 100%</p></li><li><p><strong>Benchmark</strong>: SaaS companies with 80-90% logo retention are generally in a healthy range, but higher retention is preferred for enterprise SaaS.</p></li></ul><p><strong>Churn Rate</strong></p><ul><li><p><strong>Definition:</strong> The percentage of customers or revenue lost over a given period. Effectively, churn is the inverse of retention.</p></li><li><p><strong>Formulas:</strong></p></li></ul><ol><li><p>Customer Churn = (Customers Lost During Period) / (Customers at Start of Period) * 100%</p></li><li><p>Revenue Churn = (Revenue Lost Due to Churn) / (Revenue at Start of Period) * 100%</p></li></ol><ul><li><p><strong>Benchmark</strong>: Healthy SaaS businesses aim for a monthly churn rate below 1-2% and an annual churn rate below 10-15%.</p></li></ul><p><strong>Lifetime Value of Customers (LTV)</strong></p><ul><li><p><strong>Definition:</strong> LTV estimates the total gross profit a customer generates over their lifetime with the company.</p></li><li><p><strong>Formula:</strong> LTV = (Average Revenue Per User (ARPU) × Gross Margin) / Customer Churn Rate</p></li><li><p><strong>Benchmark:</strong> A high LTV signals strong customer value; SaaS companies often target 3-5x their Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC).</p></li></ul><p><strong>Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)</strong></p><ul><li><p><strong>Definition:</strong> CAC represents the cost incurred to acquire a new customer.</p></li><li><p><strong>Formula:</strong> CAC = (Total Sales &amp; Marketing Expenses) / (Number of New Customers Acquired)</p></li><li><p><strong>Benchmark:</strong> SaaS companies should aim for efficient CAC recovery within 12 months.</p></li></ul><p><strong>LTV/CAC Ratio</strong></p><ul><li><p><strong>Definition:</strong> The ratio between customer lifetime value and customer acquisition cost, indicating profitability.</p></li><li><p><strong>Formula:</strong> LTV / CAC = Lifetime Value (LTV) / Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)</p></li><li><p><strong>Benchmark:</strong> A 3:1 or higher LTV/CAC ratio is ideal, meaning for every $1 spent acquiring a customer, the company earns $3 or more in lifetime value.</p></li></ul><h4><strong>How Customer Retention Affects Valuation</strong></h4><p>Retention metrics are critical in M&amp;A because they reflect revenue stability and growth potential. High retention rates lead to:</p><ul><li><p><strong>Higher Valuations:</strong> Investors and acquirers value SaaS companies with strong retention, as recurring revenue is more predictable and profitable.</p></li><li><p><strong>Lower Acquisition Risk:</strong> A company with high retention is less likely to face revenue volatility post-acquisition.</p></li><li><p><strong>Better Growth Multiples:</strong> Companies with high NDR (110%+) and low churn often command higher revenue multiples (10x+ ARR) compared to those with poor retention.</p></li></ul><h4><strong>Customer Retention as a Strategic Advantage</strong></h4><p>For SaaS founders, prioritizing customer retention is not only a strategy for growth—it’s also a key driver of valuation. By optimizing retention metrics, reducing churn, and maximizing customer lifetime value, founders can position their company for a successful exit at a premium multiple.</p></div></div></div>
