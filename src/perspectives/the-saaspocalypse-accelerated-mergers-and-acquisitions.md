<div class="row"><div class="col" style="--span: 12; --of: 12"><div class="b b--html"><p>If you've been watching AI disruption headlines and concluding this is a terrible time to sell, the actual deal data tells a different story.</p><p>2026 has started out significantly better than 2025, and 2025 was the strongest year for enterprise SaaS M&amp;A since 2021. Q1 of 2026 was up 78% over Q1 of 2025 and a whopping 121% from Q4 of 2025 (on a dollar volume basis, and all of this is adjusted for the $250B <a href="http://X.AI">X.AI</a> acquisition). While founders were processing AI displacement fears, buyers were executing. PE funds held approximately $2 trillion in undeployed capital as of December 2025. Enterprise SaaS was the deployment destination.</p><h4><strong>Why the Math Works in a Selloff</strong></h4><p>The mechanism is counterintuitive but not complicated. Compressed public multiples make take-privates cheaper for sponsors already holding record dry powder. When software stocks fall sharply, a sponsor's acquisition cost drops commensurately. The asset hasn't gotten worse. The price has gotten more attractive. We laid out this logic when the February selloff hit, and the full-year data confirmed it.</p><p>Both forces we've been tracking converged simultaneously: LP capital aging past deployment windows and creating urgency to act, and AI disruption giving buyers motivation to secure defensible software assets before the competitive landscape settles. When both forces point the same direction, deal activity doesn't slow. It intensifies.</p><p>Global PE deal value rose 59% in 2025 versus 2024. That number deserves a moment. In the year the SaaSpocalypse narrative hit peak cultural velocity (when the discourse was full of predictions about AI eating software) PE firms were executing at a pace unseen in years. PitchBook analysts noted that the SaaSpocalypse is accelerating M&amp;A, not slowing it down.</p><h4><strong>The Honest Caveat: This Is Not a Rising Tide</strong></h4><p>This is an era of mega deals. The <a href="http://X.AI">X.AI</a> deal alone accounted for 73% of the overall deal value for Q1 2026. The top five deals in Q1 2026 make up 89% of the total value. Seventeen mega-deals comprised more than 75% of Q4's total deal value. The volume number (245 deals in 2025) sounds broad. The value concentration says something different. Capital is not flowing indiscriminately. It's concentrating.</p><p>This is flight to quality in action. The valuation bifurcation we've been tracking (premium assets getting aggressively pursued while undifferentiated companies face compression) is exactly what the data reflects. High-quality companies with strong Rule of 40, gross dollar retention above 85%, and a defensible AI position are getting the deal activity. Companies below those thresholds are getting scrutiny, re-trades, or silence.</p><p>The SaaSpocalypse didn't kill M&amp;A. It filtered it.</p><h4><strong>What This Means Right Now</strong></h4><p>If you're a quality asset, this may be the best M&amp;A window in four years. The buyer universe is deep, dry powder is abundant and aging, strategic acquirers have returned at scale, and take-private economics remain attractive for sponsors. Every force points in the same direction.</p><p>Our own analysis of the SaaS crash dynamic has been consistent: the disruption cycle doesn't stop deal activity for quality assets, it concentrates it. Founders who move when the data is green don't wait for the narrative to catch up. The narrative rarely does.</p><p>The macro story founders have been telling themselves (too much disruption, too much uncertainty, better to wait) is not what the latest deal record reflects. What the record reflects is a market that rewarded the founders who were ready.</p><h4><strong>Bottom Line</strong></h4><p>The selloff didn't scare buyers away. It brought them in. Enterprise SaaS M&amp;A hit $83.7B in a single quarter, and 2025 was the strongest year since 2021. If you're a quality asset sitting on the sidelines waiting for a cleaner signal, you may already be looking at it.</p><h4><strong>Sources</strong></h4><p class="sqsrte-small"><strong>Fortune/PitchBook, "The Death of SaaS Could Be the Best Thing for SaaS M&amp;A," March 31, 2026</strong></p><p class="sqsrte-small"><strong>BCG, "M&amp;A Outlook 2026: Expectations Are High Again," January 2026</strong></p><p class="sqsrte-small"><strong>PitchBook Global Dry Powder Dashboard, January 2026.</strong></p><p class="sqsrte-small"><strong>Internal references: </strong><a href="http://peak-tech.com/perspectives/the-february-selloff-was-a-gift"><strong>peak-tech.com/perspectives/the-february-selloff-was-a-gift</strong></a><strong> | </strong><a href="http://peak-tech.com/perspectives/the-saaspocalypse-was-a-public-market-problem-heres-what-it-means-for-your-private-sale"><strong>peak-tech.com/perspectives/the-saaspocalypse-was-a-public-market-problem-heres-what-it-means-for-your-private-sale</strong></a><strong> | </strong><a href="http://peak-tech.com/perspectives/saas-crash-analysis"><strong>peak-tech.com/perspectives/saas-crash-analysis</strong></a></p></div></div></div>
